H-1B Economic Impact Dashboard

  • 2025–2026
  • PostgreSQL
  • Python
  • Next.js

A public dashboard of every certified H-1B application from FY2009 to FY2024, 7.4 million in all, built from sixteen years of Department of Labor files that change format almost every year. It maps where H-1B demand concentrates by state, industry and employer, tracks the COVID-era drop, and stress-tests a $100,000 per-filing fee: at FY2024 volume that is up to $51 billion a year, with IT services carrying at least 37% of it.

The most useful finding was a bug. The state chart showed Oregon's filings up 162% during COVID. Tracing it led to one column renamed in the FY2019 file, which had silently recorded 301,000 applications, 52% of the year, at the employer's headquarters instead of the worksite. National totals looked normal, which is why it was easy to miss.

Era Bias in Music Popularity Prediction

  • 2025
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • with Leila Buchan

Can a model that predicts a song's popularity from its audio features treat music from every era fairly? Across 580,000 Spotify tracks released from 1921 to 2020, a model trained on one era did worse than simply guessing the average in 19 of 20 tests on other eras.

The pooled model looked better, with an R² of 0.34. But a baseline that knows nothing about the audio and only predicts each era's average popularity scores 0.339. Nearly all of the model's accuracy came from recognizing when a song was recorded, which matters for any recommender trained mostly on recent music.

Sentiment in Peruvian Corporate Filings

  • Summer 2025
  • Python
  • Selenium
  • OCR
  • Central Reserve Bank of Peru

Built at the Central Reserve Bank of Peru to test whether the tone of corporate annual reports can serve as a leading indicator of GDP. A scraper collected up to 25 years of reports for 266 firms, replacing more than 440 hours of manual collection; OCR digitized the roughly 30% that were scanned; and a custom Spanish financial dictionary scored their sentiment.

Most of the filings proved too formulaic to carry signal, so the analysis moved to executive summaries and CEO letters. Bank CEO-letter sentiment tracked same-year growth (r ≈ 0.5, 2000–2024), a coincident signal rather than a leading one. The pipeline and documentation were handed off to the team for further work.
